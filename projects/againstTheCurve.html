At a glance: Solo developer. I built an asteroid-style arcade game in C++ with Raylib, with no game engine. I wrote the game loop, entity management, collisions, saucer AI and level progression myself.

Overview

  • Role: Solo developer.
  • Language: C++, with the Raylib library for windowing, drawing and audio.
  • Duration: about five weeks, off and on (February to March 2026).
  • Platform: Windows.

What I Built

Against The Curve is my take on the classic asteroid-style arcade game. You survive waves of incoming asteroids and hostile saucers, and the saucers shoot back, so every encounter is a test of reflexes and precision. The difficulty keeps rising with no cap, so the question is how long you can last. Raylib handles the window, drawing, input and audio, so everything else in the game is my own C++ code.

How It's Structured

The player, asteroids, bullets and saucers all inherit from one small Actor base class that holds a position and a velocity. Each kind of object has a manager class that owns its list and handles updating and drawing it, and one GameMode class owns the managers and runs each frame in order: update, check collisions, add up the score, and decide whether the level is over. The start button, the HUD and the looping music are small pieces layered on top of that.

Player Feel

The ship thrusts in whichever direction it's facing and adds that to its velocity, so it drifts the way the original does. Speed is capped, drag slowly slows it down, and it wraps around the edges of the screen. Firing gives the ship a small push backwards, and a hit sends the ship back to the center for two seconds of invincibility, shown by a colour change. There's also an emergency teleport to a random spot on the screen.

Asteroids and Levels

Each asteroid has a random size, and its speed is set from its size, so small ones move much faster than big ones. Shooting or hitting one splits it into two smaller pieces, which disappear once they get small enough. A level ends when the screen is clear of asteroids and saucers, and each level starts with two more asteroids than the last, which is where the endless difficulty curve comes from. Dying resets you to level one.

Saucers

Saucers enter from the right edge and fire at the player every few seconds. Their aim has an error that grows with their size, so large saucers are easier to dodge and the small ones are more dangerous. A small saucer arrives every ten seconds to keep the pressure on. Saucers can also be hit by asteroids and shot down.

Problem solved: player and saucer bullets share one bullet manager, so each bullet carries a tag saying who fired it. The collision checks read the tag, which means saucer shots only hurt the player and your shots only hurt saucers, without needing a second system for each side.

What I'd Improve

The ship's movement is tuned per frame instead of per second, so it only feels right at the 60 FPS the game is locked to. I'd scale it by delta time. The collision checks currently live in the bullet and asteroid managers, and I'd move them into one collision system so that adding a new object doesn't mean editing several classes. I'd also keep the score as a whole number and compare it with "at least" instead of "equal to", which is more reliable for things like extra lives.
