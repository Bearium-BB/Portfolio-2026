At a glance: Solo developer. I built a complete VR rail shooter in Unity in two to three days: spline-based movement, dual-wield shooting, enemies and scoring, for Windows and Android.

Overview

  • Role: Solo developer. I designed and programmed the whole game.
  • Engine: Unity 6, XR Interaction Toolkit and OpenXR.
  • Duration: a two to three day personal challenge.
  • Platforms: Windows and Android (standalone VR).

I set myself a challenge: build a complete VR game in two to three days that starts, plays and loops properly, instead of stopping at a rough prototype. You ride through a catacomb on rails with a gun in each hand and shoot 28 targets across three enemy types to earn your score. Everything gameplay-related is my code, and I used asset packs for the art and sound so my time could go into making it feel finished.

Rail Movement

The player is carried along a path that I drew in the editor as a spline. I wrote a custom locomotion provider for the XR Interaction Toolkit that moves the player along it at a set speed, so I could reshape the route or change the pacing without touching code. Taking movement away also keeps the player's hands free for aiming.

Shooting and Damage

Each controller holds a gun, so you dual-wield. Holding the trigger fires projectiles at a set rate, and each shot raises an event that plays the muzzle flash and a sound with slightly randomised pitch, so a long burst doesn't sound repetitive. Projectiles damage anything that implements one small interface, which means any object can become shootable without changing the weapon code.

Enemies and Scoring

Each enemy takes two hits. When one dies it adds points to the score, shows a floating "10 POINTS" label that turns to face the player, and fires an event that spawns its explosion effect and sound. I kept the enemy code the same for all three types and set the points per enemy in the scene, so scoring could be tuned without editing a script.

Built to Be Quick to Iterate

A short deadline meant I couldn't hard-code every moment of the level. Instead I wrote a few small, reusable scripts that fire Unity events, which I then connected in the editor: a trigger that fires when the player gets close, a script that eases an object to a target, and a spawner. They run the end of the level and the menu, where you start, restart or quit by shooting a target.

Problem solved: with only a few days, wiring each moment in code would have eaten the time I needed for polish. Reusable event-driven pieces let me build and change the level quickly, and the same pieces worked in several places.

What I Used From Others

The environment, weapon models, muzzle flash and sounds came from asset packs and free sound libraries, and the VR rig started from Unity's VR template. The path-following and all of the gameplay scripts on top are my own.

What I'd Improve

I'd pool the projectiles instead of creating and destroying one on every shot, since that adds up on a standalone headset. I'd also switch fast projectiles to continuous collision detection or a raycast, so a shot can't pass through a thin target.
